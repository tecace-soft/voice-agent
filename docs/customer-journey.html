Customer journey: demo to live phone line

What the product is, how the pieces fit, everything it can do, and how a business goes from its first demo to an assistant that answers its real phone. Written so someone new to the project can follow it.

In one minute

Voice Agent is an AI receptionist for small businesses. When a caller rings the business and nobody picks up (or on every call, if the business chooses), our assistant answers. It speaks with a natural voice, knows the business, and handles the call the way the owner set it up.

  • Answers questionsHours, prices, services, directions, FAQs, from the business profile.
  • Books appointmentsChecks free times and writes the booking into the business's own calendar.
  • Puts callers throughTransfers to the right person when the caller needs a human.
  • Takes messagesName, callback number and reason, saved to the dashboard and a Google Sheet.
  • Texts a linkSends a booking page or menu by SMS. Works in demos; not on the phone yet.

The business (customer)

Owns the phone line. Tries the demo and sees how it is used, sets up the assistant in the dashboard, publishes, and forwards its line to us. Reads calls and messages afterwards.

TecAce admin

Finds businesses, makes their demo, approves setup, assigns or buys a phone number, and switches the line on (Go live). Watches usage.

Callers

The business's own customers. They never see our product; they just call the business as usual and talk to the assistant.

Customers, built for hundreds

The list is meant to hold hundreds and later thousands of businesses. It had a width cap, scrolled sideways on a laptop, and had no paging. Tested with 1,200 made-up customers: no sideways or downward scroll at 1280, 1600 or 1920 pixels.

Why our calls sound choppy

A benchmark against Bland AI (reports/Bland ai 경쟁사 벤치마크.md, in Korean) found the gaps come from our call bridge, not from Twilio. No code changed yet.

What the report found

  • GPT-Live sends audio in bursts with pauses of up to 576 ms mid-sentence, longer than our 0.5 s start buffer and 0.6 s lead can cover.
  • One caller transcript fragment empties the playback buffer (_caller_started_talking), even when it is not a real interruption.
  • Model audio is dropped during the greeting window, the call nudges its own instructions mid-call, and the bridge runs one uvicorn worker.

First fixes it proposes (P1)

  • Empty the buffer only on a real barge-in signal.
  • An adaptive lead buffer, with playback tracked through Twilio mark events.
  • Pacing and conversation.item.truncate on the Realtime engine.
  • Worth an A/B on one number: OpenAI's SIP connector, which removes the bridge.

Go live checklist

  • Business information is filled in
  • Call settings are published
  • A phone number is assigned
  • Published settings work with that number
  • Calls to the number reach the receptionist (required for a number in our Twilio account; advice only for one registered by hand)
  • A number to reach the business is on file (warning only, not required)

What the admin does, in order

  1. Add the business (research optional) and work the CRM stage
  2. Send the /c/<id> demo link
  3. Approve or decline the setup request
  4. Assign a number from the pool, or buy one, inside the Go live checklist
  5. Check the list and click Go live
  6. Watch minutes and answer feedback

What happens on a live call

Once the business forwards its line, every call runs through the phone agent. The agent reads the published settings when the call is picked up, calls tools during the call, and files the call when it ends.

Words we use

Demo customer
A business we built a demo for. It gets a permanent ID like GLHF-0009.
Phase
Demo → Onboarding → Live. Stored as users.status: demo, pre-production, production.
Draft and published
Settings edits are a draft. Test calls use the draft; the real phone uses only what was published.
Call settings
Messages, transfers, links and appointment rules: the Calls group of the settings studio.
Composed session
The prompt and tools built from the settings for one call. Used by in-app and demo calls, and since 29 Sep by real calls on the GPT-Live bridge.
Rule book
The phone agent's own Python prompt. Still used by the Realtime bridge and by a business with no structured profile.
GPT-Live bridge, Realtime bridge
The two ways the phone agent can run a call. OPENAI_LIVE_MODEL set picks GPT-Live, which uses the composed session.
Guided setup
The AI consultant chat under Settings › Start here. It writes the call-settings draft and never publishes.
Agent number
The Twilio number assigned to a business. Callers never dial it directly.
Managed number
A number in our Twilio account, so the backend can check and repair its webhooks. A number registered by hand is not managed.
Webhooks
The URLs Twilio calls for a number: voice, fallback and status. They decide where a ringing call goes.
Call forwarding
The business points its own line at the agent number, for missed calls or every call.
Research
An OpenAI web search that fills a demo's profile, FAQs, hours and prompts.
Post-call review
After a demo or test call, a model reads the transcript and writes a summary, the caller's mood and what the assistant couldn't answer.
Frozen prompts
Prompts edited by hand. They stop following the profile and the FAQs until Rebuild.
My receptionist
The screens of a business still in Demo: Overview, Call activity and Settings.
Readiness checklist
What must be true before Go live: info, published settings, a number, a match, and calls that reach the receptionist.
Staging
A separate copy for testing (va-staging-backend on a Neon branch). Production deploys only from master.
